Kreis's contribution

The Robinson portrait

The special issue honored Joseph Taylor Robinson, Arkansas's senior U.S. senator and Senate majority leader. Kreis modeled the right-facing portrait used on the obverse.

His contribution was limited to this portrait side. Edward Everett Burr's eagle reverse from the earlier Arkansas Centennial type was retained for the Robinson issue.

A separate one-year type

Part of the centennial program

Congress authorized the additional design on June 26, 1936, with a maximum of 50,000 pieces. The coin belongs to the broader Arkansas Centennial program but is cataloged separately because of the Robinson portrait.

The legislation used the phrase “reverse side” for the new design. Numismatic references conventionally identify the Robinson portrait as the obverse of the issued coin.

Issue and distribution

Dated 1936, struck in 1937

The Philadelphia Mint struck 25,265 pieces in January 1937, all bearing the required 1936 date. The total includes pieces reserved for annual assay.

The coins were offered at $1.85 each, with distribution handled through Stack's in New York.
